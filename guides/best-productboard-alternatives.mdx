Productboard is the incumbent of feedback-driven roadmapping, and the reasons teams shop for alternatives are specific: per-maker pricing that gets awkward at both ends of the size curve, AI (Spark) metered by credits on top of the seat, and the insight-to-feature linking that depends on a manual habit most teams leak on. Engineering-led teams add a fourth: the whole product is shaped for PMs, and the people shipping the fixes live somewhere else.

We build Modem, one of the six below, and it competes with Productboard for part of this job, so weigh our ranking accordingly. There's a section on where Productboard still wins.

## 1. Modem

[Modem](https://modem.dev/) replaces the part of Productboard most teams actually leak on: getting feedback in and linked. Instead of makers filing insights against features, Modem reads [Slack](https://modem.dev/integrations/slack), [Discord](https://modem.dev/integrations/discord), [support](https://modem.dev/integrations/zendesk), [email](https://modem.dev/integrations/email), and [sales calls](https://modem.dev/integrations/gong) passively, clusters duplicates into topics on ingestion, and ranks them by customers affected. Topics become consolidated [Linear or Jira issues](https://modem.dev/integrations/linear) with quotes attached, or [coding-agent tasks](https://modem.dev/guides/best-tools-route-feedback-to-coding-agents), and after the fix merges it [closes the loop](https://modem.dev/close-the-loop) with the people who asked.

For the engineering-led team, that's the honest comparison: not roadmap tool vs roadmap tool, but whether you need the roadmap ceremony at all, or need the evidence flowing into the tracker your engineers already work.

**Where it fits:** engineering-led teams whose "prioritization" question is "what do we fix next and who's it for." **Where it doesn't:** no roadmap timelines, no OKRs, no stakeholder review portal; if those are load-bearing, pick from the entries below (or run Modem feeding one). Direct comparison: [Modem vs Productboard](https://modem.dev/productboard-alternative).

## 2. Jira Product Discovery

[Jira Product Discovery](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/product-discovery) is the pragmatic Jira-shop answer: idea intake, scoring, and living roadmaps that link natively to Jira delivery work, priced at $10 to $25 per creator per month with contributors free and no contributor cap even on the free tier.

There's no Spark-equivalent AI called out on the product page, and intake is manual entry inside Jira, but for an org already living in Jira it removes a vendor, a sync layer, and most of the bill.

**Where it fits:** Jira-committed orgs that mainly need intake and prioritization. See our [Productboard vs Aha! vs JPD breakdown](https://modem.dev/guides/productboard-vs-aha-vs-jira-product-discovery).

## 3. Aha! Roadmaps

[Aha!](https://www.aha.io/) is the move up rather than down: strategy, OKRs (Enterprise+), whiteboards, ideas portals, and roadmapping under one vendor, with the Elle AI assistant bundled into the seat instead of metered by credits. Premium starts at $74.59/user/month, roughly triple Productboard's entry, and Enterprise makes reviewers and viewers free.

You're trading Productboard's metered-AI-plus-maker-seat model for a higher flat seat price that buys suite breadth.

**Where it fits:** larger product orgs consolidating strategy and roadmapping under one vendor.

## 4. Canny

[Canny](https://canny.io/) replaces the customer-facing half: a public or private board where customers post, vote, and watch status change, with Autopilot capturing requests automatically from Intercom, Zendesk, Freshdesk, Gong, and Zoom, and revenue tags weighting requests by MRR/ARR. Status changes notify voters, which covers loop-closing Productboard leaves to linked-requester notifications.

It's a board, not a prioritization framework; scoring-driven orgs will miss the maker workflow.

**Where it fits:** products whose users will genuinely vote on a visible roadmap. See our [board tools comparison](https://modem.dev/guides/canny-vs-productboard-vs-featurebase).

## 5. Featurebase

[Featurebase](https://www.featurebase.app/) is the consolidation play for smaller teams: feedback board, changelog, help center, and a lightweight helpdesk in one per-seat bill from $29/seat/month, with API, MCP, and webhook access that's unusual among board tools. Its AI layer (Fibi) ships on Growth tier and above with per-resolution billing.

The board still needs a submission to exist, same as Canny and Productboard.

**Where it fits:** smaller teams that want the board and basic support tooling under one price.

## 6. Enterpret

If what you used Productboard for was Spark-style synthesis, understanding what the feedback says, more than roadmapping, [Enterpret](https://www.enterpret.com/) goes deeper on that axis alone: an adaptive taxonomy over 50+ sources with themes tied to churn, NPS, and revenue, queryable from Slack, Claude, and Cursor via its Wisdom MCP layer. Custom, demo-gated pricing.

**Where it fits:** orgs that need feedback analytics at a depth no roadmap tool reaches. See our [Modem vs Enterpret comparison](https://modem.dev/enterpret-alternative).

## Where Productboard still wins

For a product org that runs a real prioritization framework, RICE-style scoring applied by makers, tied to a Jira-synced roadmap, with Spark drafting specs and tracking launch impact inside the same tool, Productboard remains the most complete purpose-built option, and its free tier (500 notes, 25 contributors, unlimited roadmaps) is genuinely usable rather than a trial. The alternatives exist because the seat-plus-credits pricing and the manual linking step don't fit every team, not because the framework machinery is weak.

## How to choose

Name your exit reason. Maker-seat and credit math: JPD ($10/creator, contributors free) or Featurebase ($29/seat). Suite breadth: Aha!. Customer-visible roadmap: Canny. Deeper analytics: Enterpret. And if the real problem was that feedback never made it into the tool without a human filing it, and your engineers are the audience, that's the [Modem](https://modem.dev/productboard-alternative) case: capture and triage automated, output landing in the tracker, with Productboard optionally kept downstream for the roadmap ceremony.

## FAQ

### What's the best Productboard alternative for engineering teams?

The engineering-led pattern is a tracker-native stack: [Modem](https://modem.dev/) doing capture, clustering, and evidence, filing into [Linear or Jira](https://modem.dev/integrations/linear) where engineers already work, with JPD covering lightweight roadmapping if the org is on Jira. It sidesteps the maker-seat model entirely.

### Is there a free Productboard alternative?

Productboard's own free tier is decent, which sets the bar. JPD's free tier (3 creators, unlimited contributors), Canny's free tier (25 tracked users), and Modem's free tier are the real free options among the six.

### Can I keep Productboard and fix just the capture problem?

Yes, that's a common setup: Modem captures and clusters from chat, support, and calls, then the summarized topic flows into whichever roadmap tool holds the plan, Productboard included. The comparison isn't always either/or; see [Modem vs Productboard](https://modem.dev/productboard-alternative) for how the pairing works.
